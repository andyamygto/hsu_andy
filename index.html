I study electrons, phonons and the coupling between them in quantum materials, using first-principles many-body theory.

My work builds on density functional theory (DFT) and density functional perturbation theory, and goes beyond them with many-body perturbation theory: the GW approximation for quasiparticles, the Bethe–Salpeter equation (BSE) for excitons, and GW perturbation theory (GWPT) for electron–phonon coupling with self-energy effects included. I'm especially interested in how these correlation effects change superconductivity, charge-density-wave order and spectroscopic signatures, such as ARPES kinks, compared with what DFT predicts.

At USC I work with Prof. Zhenglu Li on many-body electron–phonon physics, including the kagome superconductor CsV3Sb5 and ab initio downfolding with long-range electron–phonon coupling. Before that, with Prof. Hung-Chung Hsueh at Tamkang University, I worked on first-principles phonon methods, two-dimensional materials, and charge-density waves in CuTe, together with experimental groups.

Most of my code is written in Fortran and Python.

Research

Many-body electronic structure

Quasiparticle band structures from GW and optical excitations from the BSE, and how screening shapes both.

Electron–phonon coupling & superconductivity

GWPT, anisotropic Eliashberg theory, and downfolding to effective models with both short- and long-range coupling.

Charge order & low-dimensional materials

CDW dynamics, 2D allotropes and anisotropic layered semiconductors, studied together with spectroscopy experiments.
